Senior Design Project

Lenscribe

A Real-time Speech Display for Enhanced Communication.

← Back to projects

Overview

LenScribe provides a more inclusive communication aid than traditional sound amplification. This device transcribes spoken words into text and displays them on the lens of Augmented Reality (AR) glasses for the wearer to read. The system utilizes a beamforming four microphone array and an AI-integrated speech-to-text machine (STM) to capture and process directional speech. The transcribed text will be displayed onto the AR glasses with embedded power and interface hardware.

Microphone Array

The microphone array filters out surrounding noise to capture the speaker's voice with the greatest clarity. This is done by combining the signals from four spatially separated microphones. Using a Max SNR beamforming algorithm, the array focuses on a specific direction and attentuates other sounds. This produces a narraw beam pattern that improves speech intelligibility and reduces the impact of ambient noise.

Audio Filtering

After beamforming, the analog audio signal passes through a custom two‑stage, fourth‑order Bessel low‑pass filter. Bessel filters are well suited for audio because they have a linear phase response and minimal ripple, preserving the natural quality of speech. Our design cuts off at 8 kHz to remove high‑frequency noise before sampling. 4th order Bessel topology implemented in two cascaded stages. Passband frequency of 8 kHz to cover the speech band. Linear phase response preserves intelligibility and reduces distortion.

Speech to Text

The filtered audio is digitized and processed by an automatic speech recognition (ASR) system running on a Raspberry Pi. This AI‑based engine converts speech into text in real time, enabling the captions to keep pace with a conversation.

AR Display

The final subsystem projects the transcribed text into the user's field of view. A 1 inch OLED screen renders the captions, which are then collimated and shrunk through a Galilean optical setup. A small mirror reflects the image onto a piece of semi‑transparent plastic, overlaying the text on the real world without blocking the wearer’s vision.

Glasses Design

3D printed glasses frame to house AR display and electronics. Power routed around the frame to connect to an accompanying battery pack with the Raspberry Pi. Designed to be modular, with the goal of being able to fit onto any pair of glasses.
